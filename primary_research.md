# Primary Research Synthesis — Wishlist Behaviour

> **Data Source:** Survey of 19 fashion e-commerce users  
> **Date Analysed:** September 5, 2026  
> **Objective:** Validate AI Discovery Engine hypotheses and understand the behavioural mechanics of wishlist stagnation.

---

## 1. Sample Overview

- **Total Respondents:** 19
- **Platform Usage:** 100% shop for fashion online and use a wishlist/heart feature. Platforms used include Myntra, AJIO, Nykaa Fashion, Amazon Fashion, Flipkart, and brand websites.
- **Wishlist Size:** Varies from very small (0-5 items) to very large (51-100+ items).
- **Recent Engagement:** Most users opened their wishlist *within the last week* or *today*, indicating that wishlists are highly active spaces, not forgotten archives.

---

## 2. Intent Profile: Do they actually want to buy?

When asked about the *most recent item* they added to their wishlist but haven't bought yet:
- **Majority have genuine intent:** Most users state "I still want to buy it and expect to" or "I'm considering it, but I'm not sure."
- **Age of intent:** The items have been sitting in the wishlist ranging from a few days to 1-3 months.
- **Key Insight:** The wishlist is not primarily a "graveyard." Users are actively holding high-intent items there, waiting for a catalyst to convert.

---

## 3. The Blockers: Why are they stalled?

When asked for the *main reason* in their own words, four distinct categories emerged:

### 1. Financial & Price Constraints (The "Waiters")
- *"Because I don't earn [yet]... waiting to receive money from dad"*
- *"My salary was higher in my first job; now, my salary is lower."*
- *"Waiting for sale and offers, for a reduced price point"*
- *"Out of budget"*

### 2. Information Asymmetry & Confidence Gaps (The "Hesitators")
- *"Will it be worth it!"*
- *"The product doesn't have many reviews... waiting to see if someone will be posting any"*
- *"Don't know if it will suit me... wasn't sure if it will look good on me"*
- *"I'm still unsure if it’s worth the price and whether it will actually meet my expectations"*

### 3. Availability Constraints (The "Blocked")
- *"My size is out of stock"*
- *"Size not available"*

### 4. Choice Paralysis (The "Comparers")
- *"Still comparing among other organizers"*
- *"Looking for some other options... maybe I'll find a better alternate"*

**Comparison to AI Findings:** The survey perfectly validates the AI Engine's finding that **Information/Quality Confidence** and **Price** are the leading friction points. 

---

## 4. The "Visualization" Barrier

**Question:** *Have you ever decided not to buy a wishlisted fashion item because you couldn't confidently picture how it would look or fit on you?*

- Almost every respondent answered **"Yes"** or **"Maybe"**.
- **Insight:** The inability to bridge the gap between a heavily edited product photo and personal reality is a massive, universal conversion killer. This directly confirms the "Information Asymmetry" root cause identified in the AI Engine.

---

## 5. Workarounds: What happens outside the app?

Users do not passively wait. When stalled in the evaluation stage, they actively seek information outside the shopping app. 

When considering their stalled item, respondents reported doing the following:
- **Searched YouTube** (Highly prevalent)
- **Checked Instagram / social media** (Highly prevalent)
- Read reviews elsewhere
- Checked brand websites
- Asked friends/family
- Looked for similar products on other sites
- Compared prices elsewhere

**Insight:** Myntra is losing the user's attention during the crucial evaluation phase. Because the product page lacks sufficient "real" evidence (verified photos, honest reviews), users leave the app to search YouTube and Reddit. This break in the session drastically increases the chance of abandonment.

---

## 6. Defection: How Myntra loses the sale

When users abandon a wishlisted item, what happens?
- *"I found better design and branded stuff for the same price"*
- *"Bought it from different site... the same product"*
- *"Got a notification from Flipkart that items are still in the wishlist and prices have gone down. Bought it for cheaper from somewhere else."*

**Insight:** Wishlist stagnation is a retention risk. While an item sits in the Myntra wishlist, the user is actively browsing AJIO, Amazon, and Flipkart. If a competitor offers a better price, better information, or a clearer return policy, the sale is lost.

---

## 7. Conversion Triggers: What finally breaks the stall?

When asked about items they *did* eventually buy after a long wait, the triggers were:
- **Financial unlocking:** *"When I got money"*
- **Price drops:** *"Purchased it when it came at a better price," "I got it for a discount"*
- **Stock alerts:** *"Mostly out of stock, so I bought it whenever it was available"*
- **Emotional overriding:** *"Even though I didn’t necessarily need it, I loved it so much that I felt I should have it"*

---

## 8. Strategic Conclusions for the MVP

The primary research validates the core premise of our AI Discovery Engine report: **High-intent users stall because they lack confidence, and they leave the app to find that confidence elsewhere.**

1. **Price is a constraint, but Confidence is a choice.** We cannot change a user's salary or force a 50% discount. However, we *can* solve the information gap that forces them to leave the app to search YouTube.
2. **The "Look and Fit" gap is universal.** Users explicitly state they abandon items because they cannot picture how it will look. 
3. **The MVP must bring external validation inside.** The solution must bridge the trust gap by providing the exact type of evidence users currently seek on YouTube and Reddit (real buyer photos, honest sizing feedback) directly at the point of decision.
