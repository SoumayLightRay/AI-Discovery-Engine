# Interview 1 — Transcript Analysis

> **Respondent:** Female, 23 (turning 24 in ~50 days), self-identifies as fashion person
> **Platform:** Myntra
> **Wishlist usage:** Bookmark (stated), but actual behaviour shows genuine purchase intent
> **Interview date:** Sep 5, 2026

---

## Key Findings

### 1. The Blue Cobalt Shorts Story (STRONGEST EVIDENCE IN THE PROJECT)

Two sellers selling the **same shorts** on Myntra:
- Seller A: Cheaper, no reviews
- Seller B: More expensive, good reviews

**She bought Seller B — the MORE EXPENSIVE one — because reviews gave her "surety."**

> "You wanted the surety that it had to be the same."

**Why this matters:** This single data point proves our core hypothesis more powerfully than 1,367 AI-analysed reviews. When faced with price vs. confidence, this user **chose confidence and paid more.** This directly validates that the root cause is information asymmetry, not price sensitivity.

### 2. What Prevents Purchase
- Better deal somewhere else
- Color/size not available
- Not in budget / "not worth it at this price"
- Will find a better deal later

### 3. How She Compares Products
- Reviews of the products
- Rating
- User-uploaded photos (buyer pics)
- Does NOT go to YouTube/Reddit for fashion — only compares same item on Amazon

**Nuance vs. AI data:** Our AI engine found YouTube as a major research channel. This user doesn't use YouTube for fashion research — she stays on e-commerce platforms and compares reviews. Interview 1 suggests the "research exodus" may be more about cross-platform review comparison than YouTube hauls, at least for some users.

### 4. Purchase Decision Hierarchy
Price → Occasion → Social validation → Styling → Uniqueness ("how common it is")

### 5. Feature Ideas (Unprompted)
1. **Virtual try-on** — superimpose item on your photo. Her specific insight: "Only provide this for wishlisted items, otherwise people get more indecisive and confused within choices."
2. **Influencer shots** — show famous people/Instagram influencers wearing the item, running throughout the app
3. **Top review photos** — show the best review photos on wishlisted items. Specifically top/positive reviews to boost confidence.
4. **If no reviews exist** — show reviews of the same material/fabric from the same seller

### 6. Wishlist = Bookmark, but Behaviour Says Otherwise
She stated she uses wishlist as a "bookmark." But her actual behaviour (researching, comparing, eventually purchasing the shorts) shows genuine purchase intent. This supports our survey finding that "bookmarking" and "genuine intent" overlap — the label doesn't match the action.
