# Evaluation & Grading Guidelines (NextLeap PM Fellowship)

This document distills the core feedback from the Graduation Project Feedback sessions to ensure the AI Discovery Engine submission scores in the "Top Fellow" tier (≥ 70%).

## 1. Problem Definition & Discovery
- **Customer-Centric Framing:** Define the problem based on what the user cannot do today and *why* (root cause). Avoid business-centric (e.g., "increase MAUs") or solution-led problem statements.
- **The "5 Whys":** Iterate "why" questioning to reach the actual root cause of wishlist stagnation.
- **Role of AI:** Use the AI Discovery Engine to surface patterns and opportunities. The core analytical thinking, problem structuring, and ideation MUST be done by you.

## 2. Primary Research & Segmentation
- **Derive Personas:** Create personas *after* analyzing user interviews and surveys, not beforehand.
- **Narrow Segments:** Avoid overly broad segments (e.g., 18-35 years). Choose a narrow segment and provide explicit rationale: segment size, probability of behavior change, and impact sizing.
- **Numeric Assumptions:** All numbers (e.g., metric uplifts) must have a clearly explained rationale and calculation method.

## 3. Prototyping (MVP)
- **Problem-Solution Fit First:** Do not over-invest time in building the MVP without first deeply validating the problem-solution pair.
- **Lean UX Demonstration:** If the solution is technically complex, simplify the prototype to demonstrate the core user flow and UX. Do not waste time building a full production backend.
- **Three-Level Creativity Framework:**
  1. *Level 1:* Does the solution address the identified problem?
  2. *Level 2:* Is it differentiated/novel in the market?
  3. *Level 3:* Does it provide a durable competitive advantage (proprietary data, distribution, capital moat)?

## 4. Metrics & Data Orientation
- **Explicit Definitions:** For every metric, explain *why* it was chosen, provide its exact definition (numerator/denominator), and explain how it is measured.
- **Traceability:** Connect the success metrics directly to your hypotheses and the overarching business impact (wishlist conversion).

## 5. AI Tooling Rules
- **Allowed Uses:** Drafting survey questions, scraping, data extraction (The AI Discovery Engine).
- **Prohibited Uses:** Replacing core thought, structuring the problem, ideating the solution.
- **Failure Modes:** Explicitly document AI failure modes (e.g., LLM hallucinations, variance) and your mitigation strategies. Include a token consumption/cost estimate.

## 6. Presentation & Formatting
- **Slide Titles:** The title must state the key message of the slide, not just a generic category like "Problem" or "User Research".
- **Visuals:** Put high-value wireframes and charts upfront. Avoid text-heavy slides or copy/pasted AI images. Link to supporting documents (spreadsheets/appendices).
- **Formatting Constraints:** Strictly follow formatting rules (e.g., Google Slides font size ≥ 14, Figma ≥ 26). Do not use non-standard slide sizes. Use accessible color contrasts (avoid red/green clashes).
- **Anonymity:** Redact your name from all artifacts.
