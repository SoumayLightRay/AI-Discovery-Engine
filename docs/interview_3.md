# Interview 3 — Transcript Analysis

> **Respondent:** Aziz, Male
> **Platform:** Myntra + other websites (cross-platform comparer)
> **Wishlist usage:** Comparison shortlisting — wishlists to compare across platforms
> **Interview date:** Sep 5, 2026

---

## Key Quotes

**On why items sit in wishlist:**
> "Because I was comparing that product in other websites too." (Shoes, ~2 months ago)
> Comparing: **price and reviews** across platforms for the same product.

**On paying more for confidence:**
> "No, I don't think so." — Has never paid more for better reviews/photos.
> But: "If it's a sketchy seller, then I can think about it, but it never happened to me before."

**On return policy:**
> "I never order where a website doesn't have any return policy."
> "Since I haven't seen the item in person, so I'm taking a risk, right? Maybe I may not like it when it comes to my home, so I really need to have the return policy, or at least exchange policy."

**On price vs. reviews:**
> "Of course, the price, but reviews also matter. But if it's a legit seller, then reviews might not be that much important. If I have been ordering it from the same legit seller previously also, so reviews don't matter to me at that moment."

**On buyer photos:**
> "I do check the buyer's pictures." — Prefers buyer photos over model pics.

**On what would make him instantly decide:**
> "Return policy, that's all."

---

## Key Findings

1. **Cross-platform price + review comparer** — wishlists to compare the same product across sites
2. **Return policy is his #1 decision factor** — won't order without it; unprompted, called it "taking a risk"
3. **Price > confidence for this user** — has never paid more for better reviews (counter-evidence to Int 1 & 2)
4. **Brand/seller trust substitutes for reviews** — if he trusts the seller from prior purchases, reviews become less important
5. **Does prefer buyer photos** — but not enough to pay more for them
6. **Return policy as confidence proxy** — his "return policy" need is actually about risk reduction, same root cause (information asymmetry) but different resolution mechanism
