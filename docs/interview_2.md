## Key Quotes

**On why items sit in wishlist:**
> "It's not like I don't have the money. It's just like I feel that when I wishlisted first and then I buy it a bit later, I feel more financially responsible."

> (For fashion: "I don't remember, but I usually do this.")

**On trusted brands:**

**On cross-platform comparison:**

**On the Cross-Seller Review Bridge (when directly pitched):**
> "Yeah, then maybe I would buy it." — when asked if seeing a seller's other product reviews would help her buy a zero-review item.

**On what would make her buy faster:**
> "When it become more discounted." (primary)
> "Styling options... kind of like a mood board on Pinterest, but fashion boards." (secondary)

**On buyer photos vs. model photos:**
> "I prefer a buyer's photo, but if it's a model, that's also fine."

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Wishlist as Financial Self-Regulation (NEW behaviour)
Stuti doesn't wishlist because she can't afford items — she wishlists as a **deliberate cooling-off mechanism** to feel financially responsible. This is a different motivation than price-blocked or comparison-stalled users. It's conscious accumulation control.

### 2. Zero Reviews = No Purchase
Explicitly won't buy a product with zero reviews. This directly validates the Cross-Seller Review Bridge component of our MVP — when a product has no reviews, showing the seller's other reviewed products could resolve this blocker.

### 3. Confirms Confidence > Price (again)
Paid more for the reviewed tripod over the cheaper unreviewed one. Says she "usually does this" for fashion too, even if she can't recall a specific example. Pattern matches Interview 1 (Rishika).

### 4. NO Cross-Platform Comparison
Unlike Rishika (Int 1) and the survey data (36%), Stuti does NOT compare across platforms. She's loyal to Myntra. This is a different segment — a **platform-loyal user** whose blocker is purely on-platform (reviews, confidence, price timing).

### 5. Cross-Seller Review Bridge — DIRECTLY VALIDATED
When pitched the concept ("what if something had no review, but you can check out the seller's other products, and those have good reviews?"), she said **"Yeah, then maybe I would buy it."** This is direct user validation of our novel MVP component.

### 6. Styling/Mood Board Interest
Unprompted, she asked for "styling options, like a mood board on Pinterest, but fashion boards." This aligns with the Moodboard mechanism we evaluated (and rejected via RICE) — but it's real user demand.
