## 5. Return Policy as Confidence Signal (7/11)

Not just a "nice to have" — return policy functions as a **trust proxy**:

| Name | How they use return policy |
|---|---|
| Aziz | #1 factor — "I never order where a website doesn't have any return policy" |
| Aastha | "I find it very suspicious if they don't offer return option" |
| Umesh | Fallback when no reviews |
| Steve | Checks before buying — standard practice |
| Nandini | Required alongside seller rating for zero-review products |
| Yaakrati | Safety net — "अगर return कर सकती हूँ तो मंगा के देख लूँगी" |
| Prakhar | Sees returns as "hassle, like time" — **counter-evidence** |

**Insight:** For most users, return policy = risk reduction. But Prakhar reveals the other side: returns are a hassle, so users want to be confident BEFORE buying, not rely on returns after. Both validate our root cause.

## 10. Updated Problem Statement (Post-Interviews)

### Before Interviews (Survey-based):
> Myntra's product page does not provide sufficient evidence to let users resolve their decision without leaving the platform.

### After Interviews (Validated + Refined):

> Among **high-intent wishlist users who still want their saved items** (56% of wishlisters), users who **cannot verify product quality, fit, or styling compatibility** delay purchasing because **the product page lacks real-world evidence** (buyer photos, honest fit consensus, quality proof). They currently **compare across 2–3 platforms and research on Instagram/YouTube** (10/11 users), which **delays their decision and causes 42% to buy from competitors**. Even users who cite price as their primary barrier will **pay 20–30% more when confidence signals are present** (10/11 confirmed).

## 11. The Confidence > Price Paradox

This is the core narrative for the slide deck:

```
WHAT USERS SAY:        "I'm waiting for a sale" (44%)
WHAT USERS DO:         Pay MORE for products with better reviews (91%)
WHAT THIS MEANS:       Price is the stated barrier, but confidence is the actual lever
```

The survey says price is #1. The interviews say confidence overrides price. Both are true simultaneously:

- **Price is the conscious, socially acceptable excuse** ("I'm being financially responsible")
- **Confidence is the subconscious, actual decision driver** ("I chose the expensive one because reviews were good")

This is exactly the kind of disconfirmation that Arindam's framework demands: "The story is not 'research agreed with our hypothesis.' The story is 'evidence changed our thinking.'"
